Ten days of compost at Wild Ken Hill

15 June 2026

15 to 26 June 2026, Heacham Bottom Farm, Norfolk, England

Twenty five of us spent two weeks on a 4,000 acre regenerative farm and rewilding estate on the Norfolk coast, building compost piles by hand and reading what we made under the microscope.

The group built eight piles across the ten days. Some were designed to work. Others were designed to fail, so we could watch what failure looks like and learn to turn a pile around. Everyone sourced materials, wrote their own recipes, then monitored, turned, and charted every pile through to the end. Working in small teams with a mentor meant organizing the group, splitting the turning by hand, and talking through each day's numbers together.

From the compost we made extracts and brewed tea at small and large scale, then went out into the field and practiced several ways of applying biological liquid amendments. We ran a nematode extraction on our own materials, set up protozoan infusions from what was growing locally, and plotted a sampling grid for large scale field work.

The microscope sat at the center of all of it. Beginners built confidence on the first days. Everyone else had a mentor at their shoulder naming what was on the slide, which is the fastest way we know to learn to see.

What usually takes students 18 to 24 months on their own happened in two weeks.

The workshop is a pathway through the Soil Food Web Complete Practicum, covering the learning outcomes for BioComplete™ Compost Production and Biological Liquid Amendments.
