Accelerator Workshop June 2025

Norfolk, United Kingdom · 16–27 June 2025

This workshop has ended.

In June 2025, we brought our first Accelerator Workshop to Wild Ken Hill, a 4,000-acre regenerative farm and rewilding estate on the Norfolk coast. It is home to Nick Padwick, who has managed the estate since 2018 and was the first British farmer to qualify as a Soil Food Web consultant.

For ten days, students worked in small teams with our mentors, building compost piles and nursing them along. Some piles were designed to fail, so students could practice rescuing them. Piles heat up on their own schedule, so the timetable moved with them almost every day. Every extract, tea and sample went under the microscope.

Student Melita Coggins called it a "fantastic way to gain a huge amount of experience with different piles, in a very short amount of time."

Nick's fields were part of the classroom, and his story is worth telling. He grew up on a farm in Hampshire and has more than 30 harvests behind him. In 2009, Farmers Weekly named him both Farm Manager of the Year and Farmer of the Year. When he arrived at Ken Hill, he described the soil as "pretty dull," with hardly any life in it. In 2020 he took his first course with Dr. Elaine Ingham, and he went on to qualify as a Soil Food Web consultant, one of only 39 in the world at the time.

Today he produces around 750 tons of compost a year, and it has gone onto all of the estate's farmland. Within a year, the fungi in his soil grew from barely there to more than half the level of the bacteria, and he is aiming for an even balance. The farm has grown its crops without insecticide for more than a decade and is converting its arable land to organic. In 2022, an independent assessment placed it in the top 5 percent of UK arable farms for carbon sequestration. In his words: "We want to share what we're doing here so that we can help UK agriculture make the changes needed, at pace."

Students come from all over the world and take what they learn back to their own corner of the globe. They left Wild Ken Hill with the same skills Nick used to bring his "pretty dull" soil back to life.
