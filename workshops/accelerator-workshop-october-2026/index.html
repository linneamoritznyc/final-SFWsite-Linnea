Accelerator Workshop October 2026

Tamil Nadu, India · 19–30 October 2026

A year's worth of hands-on experience in ten days

In October 2026 the Soil Food Web Foundation brings its Accelerator Workshop to Tamil Nadu, in southern India, in collaboration with Isha Outreach (India workshop page). For ten days, students build BioComplete™ compost, create biological liquid amendments and practise microscopy alongside experienced mentors and an international community (India workshop page).

The promise is simple: a year's worth of hands-on experience in one immersive workshop (India workshop page). What often takes students 18 to 24 months, building multiple compost piles alone, they can achieve here in a focused, supportive environment in just two weeks (India workshop page).

Where students learn

The workshop is run with the Isha Foundation and the Conscious Planet Save Soil movement (India workshop page). Students stay at the Isha Yoga Center, at the foothills of the Velliangiri Mountains about 30 km west of Coimbatore, where accommodation and vegetarian meals are part of the workshop (India workshop page). Each day a shuttle takes them about 3 km to Sadhguru's Save Soil Regenerative Revolution Farm, which serves as the outdoor classroom (India workshop page).

The farm itself is part of the lesson. Sadhguru established it in 2007 as part of Isha's commitment to environmental restoration, and it works to improve soil fertility while improving the livelihoods of farmers throughout Tamil Nadu and Puducherry (India workshop page). It is a living demonstration farm for regenerative agriculture and soil restoration, using research and farmer-to-farmer education to change farming across the region (India workshop page).

What students do

Students work in small groups, each with an assigned instructor who reviews their compost recipes and follows every pile with them (India workshop page).

  • Week one: compost. Students select, source and store feedstocks, work out carbon-to-nitrogen ratios and write custom recipes using the SFW Composting Workbook. Then they build multiple thermophilic compost piles from scratch and hydrate, monitor, turn and troubleshoot each one (India workshop page). Because several piles are built at the same time, students see how different recipes and decisions change the composting process in the critical first ten days (India workshop page).
  • Week two: liquid amendments and the field. Students produce and assess compost extracts, compost teas, nematode extractions and protozoan infusions. They also collect soil samples, assess fields, design experiments and practise applying amendments in the field (India workshop page).
  • Microscopy throughout. Students repeatedly assess soil, compost, extracts, teas, infusions and more under the microscope, so they learn to connect what they see on the slide with what is happening in the pile and the field (India workshop page). Beginner microscopy training is available for anyone who needs extra support (India workshop page).

Together, these activities cover all the learning objectives for the BioComplete™ Compost Production and Biological Liquid Amendments courses (India workshop page). The workshop is an alternative, equivalent pathway through the Soil Food Web Complete Practicum, designed for students who learn best by doing (India workshop page). Students who attend all ten days, complete the four Foundation Courses and the Soil Microscopy Course, and finish the pre-workshop course and post-workshop assessment can graduate both courses (India workshop page).

The workshop is also open to people who are new to our courses. Participants can qualify either by completing the Foundation Courses or by attending the two-day Life in the Soils seminar (India workshop page), held at the same Save Soil Farm on 16 and 17 October (Life in the Soils seminar page). That opens the door to local farmers and growers who have never taken an online course.

The team

The instructors are Loida Vasquez, Director of Advanced Education; Gerald Ramírez, mentor and agronomist; Evan Buckman, Executive Director of the Soil Food Web Foundation; and Kavi Reddy, who leads growth, partnerships and permaculture (India workshop page). Students get two weeks of live access to Soil Food Web mentors, executives and curriculum creators, with one-on-one and group Q&A (India workshop page).

Why it matters

The Foundation's promise is "Healing soil. Feeding humanity. Restoring the living world" (Soil Food Web Foundation), and in India, soil and food security are tightly linked. India has more undernourished people than any other country: more than 172 million in 2024, about 12 percent of the population (Down To Earth). 18.7 percent of Indian children under five suffer from wasting, the highest rate in the world (Down To Earth). At the same time, 29.7 percent of India's land was degraded in 2018 to 2019, up from 28.76 percent in 2003 to 2005 (Down To Earth).

The soil itself is running low on life. Of about 13 million soil samples tested through India's Soil Health Card program in 2023 to 2025, 48.5 percent were low in organic carbon (Down To Earth). Tamil Nadu ranks among the five Indian states with the most organic carbon deficient soils (Down To Earth). The country fills the gap with chemistry, at great public cost: the 2026 to 2027 Union Budget set aside 1.168 trillion rupees, about 12.75 billion US dollars, for urea subsidies alone (Argus Media).

Tamil Nadu is also where a different path is already taking root. Agricultural scientist G. Nammalvar pioneered the organic farming movement in the state and trained farmers until his death in 2013 (Down To Earth). In March 2023 the state government unveiled an Organic Farming Policy aiming for chemical-free agriculture (The South First). Our partners are part of this. Save Soil's Regenerative Revolution program promotes "soil biology-based regenerative agriculture," runs a model farm in Semmedu near Coimbatore, and reports more than 33,000 farmers trained (Conscious Planet). Near the Isha Yoga Center, the Velliangiri Uzhavan farmer producer organization, supported by Isha, grew to 1,063 farmers with a turnover of 17.7 crore rupees in 2021 to 2022 (Conscious Planet). The Accelerator Workshop adds the science and the hands-on skill to see soil life under a microscope and grow it in a compost pile, so this movement can measure what it builds.

The Save Soil movement itself was launched by Sadhguru to turn soil loss around through national soil policy (Conscious Planet). In 2022 he rode a motorcycle alone for 100 days and 30,000 km, from London to India through 27 nations (Conscious Planet), and the campaign reached over 3.9 billion people, with 74 nations offering support (Sadhguru blog). The movement asks governments to require a minimum of 3 to 6 percent organic content in agricultural soils, and Gujarat became the first Indian state to sign a Save Soil agreement (Free Press Journal).

Across India, farming that works with soil biology is already proving itself at scale. Andhra Pradesh's Community Managed Natural Farming programme (APCNF) (Instagram @apzbnf) has reached 1.8 million farmers and won the 2026 Food Planet Prize (Food Planet Prize). The UN Environment Programme reports at least a 30 percent increase in productivity and income for farmers who adopted it (UNEP). A 2026 analysis of the programme's soil science builds on Dr. Elaine Ingham's soil food web research (Biodiversity for a Livable Climate), and Didi Pershouse, who teaches the school's Soil Sponge Regeneration Workshop, has worked as a trainer and curriculum developer for APCNF (Soil Sponge Regeneration Workshop page).

Every student who leaves this workshop goes home able to make living compost and liquid amendments from local materials and to check the results under a microscope. In a country where so much depends on the soil, each of those people can help the farms around them rely less on bought-in chemistry.
