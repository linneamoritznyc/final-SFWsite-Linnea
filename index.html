WHO WE ARE

A Foundation built on living soil

For over four decades, Dr. Elaine Ingham showed the world what farmers and foresters have always sensed: soil is not dirt. It is a living web of bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, and microarthropods that feeds plants, stores carbon, holds water, and sustains every terrestrial ecosystem on Earth.

The Soil Food Web Foundation was created to carry that work forward. We teach the science through the Soil Food Web School, pursue and share new knowledge, incubate regenerative practice in ecoregions around the world, and build the community that makes it all last.

THE SOIL FOOD WEB APPROCH

Partner with nature's intelligence

  • Observe and assess

    Put your soil under the microscope. Identify which organisms are present, which are missing, and what that means for your plants — so every decision starts with what’s actually alive in your soil.

  • Incubate the biology

    Grow the beneficial organisms your soil needs by making BioComplete™ compost — not just organic matter, but a living culture, built deliberately.

  • Multiply and apply

    A small amount of biologically rich compost goes a long way. Compost extracts and teas multiply that life and carry it across whole fields and gardens — to the soil and to the plants themselves.

  • Manage and monitor holistically

    Keep observing as the life rebuilds structure, cycles nutrients, and protects your plants — and adjust your practices so the benefits compound, season after season.

    Foundation Courses

    Build your living soil foundational knowledge as we take you through the 4 foundational courses: Foundation Course 1: Introduction to the Soil Food Web Foundation Course 2: BioComplete™ Compost Foundation Course 3: BioComplete™ Liquid Amendments Foundation Course 4: Microscopy

  • From Curious to Consultant

    Complete Practicum

    Begin with all four of the foundation courses to gain the foundational knowledge needed to understand the role and importance of the soil food web, as well as why microscopy is the best analysis method when the goal is efficient identification and population counts for the main beneficial soil microbes in the soil food web. Then move on to gaining practical hands-on skills in all four of our Advanced programs.

    Learn more

  • Hone your skills

    Soil Microscopy

    Seeing is believing, and when it comes to the regeneration of living soil, a microscope is your most powerful ally: Become a Soil Food Web Lab Tech, See what your soil really needs before spending time and money to improve it, Measure the real impact of your efforts near instantly, No more sending samples across the country for assessment | Complete on-site assessments yourself

    Learn more

  • Hone your skills

    BioComplete™ Compost Production

    Learn to produce biologically complete compost that restores soil life, suppresses disease, and transforms what’s possible on your land. Discover how to redirect organic waste into a biologically rich amendment, grow the right microbes to support healthy soil and plants while minimizing the risk of human pathogens and weed seeds, and replace expensive, harmful chemical inputs with the power of biology and organic matter.

    Learn more

  • 12 weeks | 2 piles of BioComplete Compost

    Compost Intensive Course

    The Compost Intensive Course is a hands-on, 10-week online training designed to guide you through the process from beginning to end alongside a global cohort of peers. There are NO prerequisites! And it counts toward your consultant pathway*

    Learn more

  • Hone your skills

    Learn the Art and Science of Biological Liquid Amendments

    Build on your Soil Food Web Microscopy and BioComplete™ Compost Production skills by learning to purposefully breed, multiply, and extract key microbes for various real world field application needs

    Advanced Field Trial

    You've learned to see life in a microscope slide. You've built biologically complete compost from the ground up. You've brewed and applied biological liquid amendments. You understand, at a cellular level, what healthy soil looks like and why it matters. Now it's time to prove it in the field.
