About the Foundation

In service to the soil that sustains us all

The Soil Food Web Foundation is a nonprofit organization carrying forward the life's work of Dr. Elaine Ingham, empowering communities everywhere to restore and protect the Earth's soil and living systems.

Our Mission

To honor and grow Dr. Elaine Ingham's legacy of empowering communities to restore and protect the Earth's soil and living systems by sharing, teaching, and learning about the soil food web, pursuing knowledge related to soil, plant, and planetary health, incubating regenerative agricultural and ecosystem practices, and building community and financial support for soil food web teaching, research, and practice.

Our Vision

Healthy, living soil in every terrestrial ecoregion on the planet that is nurtured by local communities who understand why soil health is the foundation of ecosystem, agricultural, planetary, and human wellbeing.

The Soil Food Web School is a program of the Soil Food Web Foundation. Course fees are program revenue; after the cost of delivering the courses they fund scholarships, open research and field projects. How we’re funded

Our history

Rooted in legacy, growing in impact

  1. 1981

    Ph.D., Colorado State University

    Elaine Ingham earns her Ph.D. in soil microbiology at Colorado State University, following a B.A., cum laude, from St. Olaf College (1974) and an M.S. in microbiology from Texas A&M University. Her first scientific papers appear in 1982, beginning with work on ultraviolet-induced autofluorescence of mycorrhizal root infections.

  2. 1985

    The foundational paper

    She co-authors "Interactions of Bacteria, Fungi, and their Nematode Grazers: Effects on Nutrient Cycling and Plant Growth" (Ecological Monographs 55:119–140), detailing the relationships between soil food web trophic groups and their role in plant nutrition.

  3. 1985–2002

    Research and teaching

    Postdoctoral research at the University of Georgia's Institute of Ecology, then the faculty of Oregon State University (1986–2002) as Instructor and Associate Professor. She serves as the first President of the Soil Ecology Society, as Program Chair of the Ecological Society of America, and co-authors the USDA's Soil Biology Primer (1999). Across her career: more than seventy scientific papers, six book chapters, and seventeen technical reports.

  4. 1996

    Soil Foodweb Inc.

    She founds Soil Foodweb Inc., bringing microscopy-based soil assessment to growers through soil-testing laboratories, later expanding through the Soil Foodweb Institute in Australia.

  5. 2010–2013

    Teaching and research leadership

    She teaches at Maharishi University of Management (Maharishi International University) (2010–2013) and serves as Chief Scientist at the Rodale Institute (2011).

  6. 2014

    Environmental Celebration Institute

    She establishes the Environmental Celebration Institute, a farm and research facility in Northern California where she directs research and teaches in person.

  7. 2019

    The Soil Food Web School

    She creates the Soil Food Web School, bringing her curriculum online. Enrollment grows to more than 10,000 students across 100+ countries.

  8. 2025

    The Soil Food Web Foundation

    As Dr. Ingham retires from teaching, the Foundation is established to continue her work, holding the school's programs and intellectual property, with a broader mission of open research, applied practice, and community support.

  9. 2026

    Carrying the work forward

    Dr. Ingham passed away on February 16, 2026. The Foundation continues the work, dedicated to implementing broadly what Elaine proved and demonstrated throughout her lifetime.

What we do

Four ways we grow this work

  • Teaching & learning

    World-class education in soil biology, from foundation courses to professional certification, with workshops in a growing list of ecoregions and support that makes learning accessible.

  • Pursuing knowledge

    Replicating key experiments, opening our trial and case-study data, and advancing microscopy and lab methods, so more people understand why the soil food web works.

  • Incubating applied practice

    Ecoregion hubs that integrate education, composting, microscopy, and consulting; partnerships with farmers to trial and document soil food web practices where they live.

  • Building community & support

    A global network of students, volunteers, donors, and advisors who add their support to increase accessibility, resilience, and depth of knowledge to our urgent mission.

Dr. Elaine's research

The woman who started it all

Dr. Elaine Ingham pioneered research in soil biology across the final four decades of her life and made her science radically accessible, so that anyone, anywhere, could learn to restore the soil beneath their feet.

This Foundation is her living legacy, and we hold it with care: not by asking the world to take her word for it, but by opening her science for the world to prove.
