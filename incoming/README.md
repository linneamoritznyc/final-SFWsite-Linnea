# Incoming

Drop anything here: photographs, clips, in whatever format came off the phone or
out of the Drive. Mixed types are fine and sorting is not your job.

This is a staging area, not a home. Files here have not been named, sized,
converted, credited or chosen. They get moved out to where they belong and this
folder goes back to empty. A file sitting here is a file nobody has looked at
yet.

## Where things end up

| What | Where it lands | What happens to it |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Photographs | `img/` | Renamed for what is in them, then `tools/images.py` writes the 1600 and 800 pixel versions into `img/w/` that the pages load |
| Clips | `video/`, or `video/<story>/` for a set belonging to one story | Converted to MP4 and WebM at a sensible bitrate, with a poster frame saved beside them |
| Openly licensed work by other people | `img/pd/` | Different rules, its own README, a credit line per file |

The originals stay in the repository at full size. The pages never load them.

## What to send with a file

The file on its own is only half of it. A photograph of real people at a real
place needs to say where it was, when, and who is in it, and none of that can be
read off the picture. What the Foundation knows, the caption can say. What it
does not know, the caption leaves out rather than guessing.

So alongside the upload, as loosely as you like:

- **What it shows.** "Compost turning at the March workshop" is plenty.
- **Where and when**, if known.
- **Who is in it**, and whether they agreed to appear on a public website. This
  matters most for anything with faces, and most of all for photographs of
  students.
- **Who took it**, if the Foundation needs to credit them.

Filenames like `Teas(6).mov` or `IMG_5740.HEIC` carry none of this, which is
fine, but it does mean the note has to.

## Formats

**HEIC and MOV are welcome.** They come straight off an iPhone and no browser
plays or shows them, so they are converted on arrival: MOV and MP4 to a web MP4,
HEIC to JPEG. Send the originals rather than exporting first, so the conversion
happens once from the best copy.

Very large files are fine too. A clip is re-encoded down for the web and the
original is what that is done from.

## Permission

These are the Foundation's own photographs and the Foundation's own people.
Anything that arrives here is treated as not yet cleared for publication until
somebody says otherwise. Nothing goes on a public page on the strength of having
been uploaded.
