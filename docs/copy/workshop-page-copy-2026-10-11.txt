A year's worth of hands-on experience in ten days
In October 2026 the Soil Food Web Foundation brings its Accelerator Workshop to Tamil Nadu, in southern India, in collaboration with Isha Outreach ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)). <mark>[CONFIRM with Evan: first ever in India.]</mark> For ten days, students build BioComplete™ compost, create biological liquid amendments and practise microscopy alongside experienced mentors and an international community ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)).
The promise is simple: a year's worth of hands-on experience in one immersive workshop ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)). What often takes students 18 to 24 months, building multiple compost piles alone, they can achieve here in a focused, supportive environment in just two weeks ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)).
Where students learn
The workshop is run with the [Isha Foundation](https://isha.sadhguru.org/) and the [Conscious Planet Save Soil](https://consciousplanet.org/) movement ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)). Students stay at the Isha Yoga Center, at the foothills of the Velliangiri Mountains about 30 km west of Coimbatore, where accommodation and vegetarian meals are part of the workshop ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)). Each day a shuttle takes them about 3 km to Sadhguru's Save Soil Regenerative Revolution Farm, which serves as the outdoor classroom ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)).
The farm itself is part of the lesson. Sadhguru established it in 2007 as part of Isha's commitment to environmental restoration, and it works to improve soil fertility while improving the livelihoods of farmers throughout Tamil Nadu and Puducherry ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)). It is a living demonstration farm for regenerative agriculture and soil restoration, using research and farmer-to-farmer education to change farming across the region ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)).
What students do
Students work in small groups, each with an assigned instructor who reviews their compost recipes and follows every pile with them ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)).

* Week one: compost. Students select, source and store feedstocks, work out carbon-to-nitrogen ratios and write custom recipes using the SFW Composting Workbook. Then they build multiple thermophilic compost piles from scratch and hydrate, monitor, turn and troubleshoot each one ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)). Because several piles are built at the same time, students see how different recipes and decisions change the composting process in the critical first ten days ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)).
* Week two: liquid amendments and the field. Students produce and assess compost extracts, compost teas, nematode extractions and protozoan infusions. They also collect soil samples, assess fields, design experiments and practise applying amendments in the field ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)).
* Microscopy throughout. Students repeatedly assess soil, compost, extracts, teas, infusions and more under the microscope, so they learn to connect what they see on the slide with what is happening in the pile and the field ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)). Beginner microscopy training is available for anyone who needs extra support ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)).

Together, these activities cover all the learning objectives for the BioComplete™ Compost Production and Biological Liquid Amendments courses ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)). The workshop is an alternative, equivalent pathway through the Soil Food Web Complete Practicum, designed for students who learn best by doing ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)). Students who attend all ten days, complete the four Foundation Courses and the Soil Microscopy Course, and finish the pre-workshop course and post-workshop assessment can graduate both courses ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)).
The workshop is also open to people who are new to our courses. Participants can qualify either by completing the Foundation Courses or by attending the two-day Life in the Soils seminar ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)), held at the same Save Soil Farm on 16 and 17 October ([Life in the Soils seminar page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/life-in-the-soils-seminar-2-day)). That opens the door to local farmers and growers who have never taken an online course.
The team
The instructors are Loida Vasquez, Director of Advanced Education; Gerald Ramírez, mentor and agronomist; Evan Buckman, Executive Director of the Soil Food Web Foundation; and Kavi Reddy, who leads growth, partnerships and permaculture ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)). Students get two weeks of live access to Soil Food Web mentors, executives and curriculum creators, with one-on-one and group Q&A ([India workshop page](https://school.soilfoodweb.com/courses/india-workshop-2026)).
Why it matters
The Foundation's promise is "Healing soil. Feeding humanity. Restoring the living world" ([Soil Food Web Foundation](https://new.soilfoodweb.com/?p=321)), and in India, soil and food security are tightly linked. India has more undernourished people than any other country: more than 172 million in 2024, about 12 percent of the population ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/food/india-has-highest-rate-of-wasting-in-the-world-despite-30-decrease-in-undernourishment-since-2006-un-food-security-nutrition-report)). 18.7 percent of Indian children under five suffer from wasting, the highest rate in the world ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/food/india-has-highest-rate-of-wasting-in-the-world-despite-30-decrease-in-undernourishment-since-2006-un-food-security-nutrition-report)). At the same time, 29.7 percent of India's land was degraded in 2018 to 2019, up from 28.76 percent in 2003 to 2005 ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/climate-change/despite-pm-modi-s-assurance-land-degradation-desertification-increasing-78586)).
The soil itself is running low on life. Of about 13 million soil samples tested through India's Soil Health Card program in 2023 to 2025, 48.5 percent were low in organic carbon ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/food/cse-assessment-finds-indian-soils-severely-deficient-in-key-nutrients)). Tamil Nadu ranks among the five Indian states with the most organic carbon deficient soils ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/how-nutrient-deficient-are-indian-soils--82732)). The country fills the gap with chemistry, at great public cost: the 2026 to 2027 Union Budget set aside 1.168 trillion rupees, about 12.75 billion US dollars, for urea subsidies alone ([Argus Media](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2783524-india-outlines-2026-27-urea-subsidy)).
Tamil Nadu is also where a different path is already taking root. Agricultural scientist G. Nammalvar pioneered the organic farming movement in the state and trained farmers until his death in 2013 ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/environment/remembering-nammalvar-farewell-organic-saintwarrior--43172)). In March 2023 the state government unveiled an Organic Farming Policy aiming for chemical-free agriculture ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/pti/tamil-nadu-rolls-out-organic-farming-policy-for-chemical-free-agriculture/)). Our partners are part of this. Save Soil's Regenerative Revolution program promotes "soil biology-based regenerative agriculture," runs a model farm in Semmedu near Coimbatore, and reports more than 33,000 farmers trained ([Conscious Planet](https://consciousplanet.org/en/save-soil-regenerative-revolution)). Near the Isha Yoga Center, the Velliangiri Uzhavan farmer producer organization, supported by Isha, grew to 1,063 farmers with a turnover of 17.7 crore rupees in 2021 to 2022 ([Conscious Planet](https://consciousplanet.org/en/cauvery-calling/blog/rise-and-rise-of-velliangiri-uzhavan-fpo)). The Accelerator Workshop adds the science and the hands-on skill to see soil life under a microscope and grow it in a compost pile, so this movement can measure what it builds.
The Save Soil movement itself was launched by Sadhguru to turn soil loss around through national soil policy ([Conscious Planet](https://consciousplanet.org/)). In 2022 he rode a motorcycle alone for 100 days and 30,000 km, from London to India through 27 nations ([Conscious Planet](https://consciousplanet.org/press-releases/sadhguru-kicks-off-100-day-motorcycle-journey-from-london-to-save-soil)), and the campaign reached over 3.9 billion people, with 74 nations offering support ([Sadhguru blog](https://innerengineering.sadhguru.org/online/blog/riding-to-save-soil-an-epic-journey)). The movement asks governments to require a minimum of 3 to 6 percent organic content in agricultural soils, and Gujarat became the first Indian state to sign a Save Soil agreement ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/india/gujarat-becomes-first-indian-state-to-sign-mou-with-isha-outreach-to-save-soil)).
Across India, farming that works with soil biology is already proving itself at scale. Andhra Pradesh's [Community Managed Natural Farming programme (APCNF)](https://apcnf.in/) (Instagram [@apzbnf](https://www.instagram.com/apzbnf/)) has reached 1.8 million farmers and won the 2026 Food Planet Prize ([Food Planet Prize](https://foodplanetprize.org/press/apcnf-wins-the-2026-food-planet-prize/)). The UN Environment Programme reports at least a 30 percent increase in productivity and income for farmers who adopted it ([UNEP](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/story/how-natural-farming-transforming-food-production-southern-india)). A 2026 analysis of the programme's soil science builds on Dr. Elaine Ingham's soil food web research ([Biodiversity for a Livable Climate](https://bio4climate.org/2026/08/12/the-living-soil-a-holistic-analysis-of-nutrient-dynamics-in-andhra-pradesh-community-managed-natural-farming-apcnf/)), and Didi Pershouse, who teaches the school's Soil Sponge Regeneration Workshop, has worked as a trainer and curriculum developer for APCNF ([Soil Sponge Regeneration Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/soil-sponge-regeneration-workshop/)).
Every student who leaves this workshop goes home able to make living compost and liquid amendments from local materials and to check the results under a microscope. In a country where so much depends on the soil, each of those people can help the farms around them rely less on bought-in chemistry. <mark>[FIND: public link for the free Voices of Regeneration panel in Hyderabad on 10 October.]</mark> <mark>[FIND: how many students are from India, and from which states.]</mark>

Where regenerative farming meets rewilding
In June 2026 the Soil Food Web Foundation returned to [Wild Ken Hill](https://wildkenhill.co.uk/about/), a 4,000-acre regenerative farm and rewilding estate on the Norfolk coast, about three hours from London ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). It was our second workshop there, one year after June 2025 ([SFW workshops page](https://new.soilfoodweb.com/workshops/)). All 25 places sold out ([June 2026 workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/?p=95476)).
Wild Ken Hill uses rewilding, regenerative farming and traditional conservation side by side. Its land stretches from the sea through coastal scrub, freshwater marshes, heathland and wood pasture to nature-rich farmland, and more than 2,500 species have been recorded there ([UK Accelerator Workshop page, FAQ](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). The estate also runs Soil Food Web based composting on its farmland ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)), so students learned on land where the method is already at work.
What the workshop was for
The Accelerator Workshop is the fastest way through the hands-on part of our advanced training. What often takes students 18 to 24 months, building multiple compost piles alone, they did here in two weeks, in a focused and supportive environment ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). The ten days covered all the learning outcomes for the BioComplete™ Compost Production and Biological Liquid Amendments courses ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). Students who attended all ten days, completed the four Foundation Courses and the Soil Microscopy Course, and submitted their learning objectives document could graduate both courses ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)).
The workshop was open to all current SFW students who had completed Foundation Course 3 and watched the lectures in Foundation Course 4, plus an online pre-workshop training course ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). That preparation meant students arrived ready to work.
What students did
Students worked in teams of four or five, each with its own instructor who reviewed their recipes and followed the trajectory of every pile with them ([UK Accelerator Workshop page, FAQ](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). Over ten days the group built and monitored eight compost piles ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). <mark>[CONFIRM, Linnea: number of teams, and how the eight piles were split between them.]</mark> With eight piles side by side, every student could see how different recipes, feedstocks and management styles played out in real time ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)).
The workshop was built so students would engage all their senses: they see, feel, hear and smell what correct composting is like ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). Over the two weeks they also:

* Learned to source and store compost materials under different constraints ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/))
* Organized group compost building and turning by hand, which builds leadership skills ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/))
* Diagnosed failing piles in real time and learned how to turn one around ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/))
* Made compost extracts with several methods and brewed compost tea at small and large scale ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/))
* Practised several field application methods for liquid amendments ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/))
* Ran a nematode extraction from the materials they had helped produce, and made a protozoan infusion from local materials ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/))
* Plotted a sampling grid to practise large-scale field work ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/))
* Used applied microscopy on soil, compost, extracts, teas, nematode extractions and protozoan infusions, with an expert beside them ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/))

The training team brought a wide mix of skills, including farming, biology, agronomy and permaculture ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)).

Why it matters
England has set itself a deadline for its soils. The government's revised Environmental Improvement Plan aims to bring at least 40 percent of England's agricultural soil into sustainable management by 2028, and 60 percent by 2030 ([AHDB](https://ahdb.org.uk/the-revised-environmental-improvement-plan)). Farmers are already being paid to start: by October 2025, soil management actions under the Sustainable Farming Incentive covered 44 percent of England's farmed area ([Defra](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/sustainable-farming-incentive-action-uptake-data-october-2025/sustainable-farming-incentive-action-uptake-data-october-2025)). Targets like these need people who can read what is happening in a soil and know how to bring it back to life. That is the skill the Accelerator Workshop teaches.
Wild Ken Hill shows that farming and wildlife can recover on the same land. Since 2019 the estate has rewilded about 1,000 acres of poor-quality farmland, and within three years the average number of plant species in its survey plots roughly doubled, from 16.8 to 33.2 ([Rewilding Britain](https://rewildingbritain.org.uk/rewilding-projects/wild-ken-hill); [BirdGuides](https://www.birdguides.com/news/plant-numbers-double-at-wild-ken-hill/)). The farmed land is thriving too. Living soil in the high desert
The February 2026 Accelerator Workshop took place at [Synergia Ranch](https://synergiaranch.com/), about 15 miles south of Santa Fe, New Mexico ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synergia_Ranch)). It was our dryland workshop, held where water is scarce and every bit of soil organic matter counts.
The ranch has a remarkable history. It was founded in 1969 by John P. Allen, who in 1984 launched the Biosphere 2 project with Ed Bass ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synergia_Ranch)). The ranch covers 111 acres, and its orchard grows on high-desert clay at about 6,300 feet; since 2019 the farm has used a no-till approach ([Institute of Ecotechnics](https://ecotechnics.edu/synergia-ranch-old/)). When the orchard was planted, a compost pit three feet wide and three feet deep was dug under every tree ([Institute of Ecotechnics](https://ecotechnics.edu/synergia-ranch-old/)). Decades of that care show in the soil: recent tests found 4.5 to 5.5 percent organic matter in the farm's soils, against 1.3 percent in soils nearby ([Institute of Ecotechnics](https://ecotechnics.edu/synergia-ranch-old/)). Students learned to build soil life on a farm that has already shown how much it can change.
What students did
The Accelerator Workshop gives students the experience of building and managing multiple compost piles in two weeks, work that often takes 18 to 24 months alone ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). In the standard format, students work in small teams with a mentor, build BioComplete™ compost piles from scratch, monitor and turn them, make compost extracts and teas, run nematode extractions and protozoan infusions, and assess everything they make under the microscope ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). Together these cover the learning outcomes for the BioComplete™ Compost Production and Biological Liquid Amendments courses ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)). <mark>[CONFIRM: number of piles built and the full list of what students practised in Santa Fe.]</mark>

Why it matters
This region is running short of water. The southwestern megadrought from 2000 to 2021 was the driest 22-year period in the region since at least the year 800, and climate change accounts for about 42 percent of its soil moisture deficit ([Columbia Climate School](https://news.climate.columbia.edu/2022/02/14/megadrought-in-southwest-is-now-the-worst-in-at-least-1200-years-study-confirms/)). In July 2022 the Rio Grande ran dry in Albuquerque for the first time in 40 years ([Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/rio-grande-runs-dry-in-albuquerque-180980484/)).
In dry country, soil organic matter works like a reservoir. Each 1 percent increase in organic matter lets the top foot of soil hold about 3,400 more gallons of plant-available water per acre ([University of Nebraska-Lincoln Water](https://water.unl.edu/article/animal-manure-management/connection-between-soil-organic-matter-and-soil-water/)). By that rule of thumb, the gap between Synergia's soils and its neighbors' is worth thousands of extra gallons of water per acre ([Institute of Ecotechnics](https://ecotechnics.edu/synergia-ranch-old/); [University of Nebraska-Lincoln Water](https://water.unl.edu/article/animal-manure-management/connection-between-soil-organic-matter-and-soil-water/)). Living soil also holds itself in place: US Geological Survey research on the Colorado Plateau finds that dust emissions rise during drought, and that biological soil covers are critical to keeping desert soils stable ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/southwest-biological-science-center/science/wind-erosion-and-dust-emissions-colorado)).
The need on the food side is just as clear. About one in six New Mexicans faces food insecurity, and the state has the fourth highest rate of child food insecurity in the country ([Roadrunner Food Bank](https://www.rrfb.org/learn/hunger-research/map-the-meal-gap/)). New Mexico is investing in the answer: its Healthy Soil Program has awarded more than 2.4 million dollars to 158 on-the-ground projects ([New Mexico Department of Agriculture](https://nmdeptag.nmsu.edu/new-release/2024/land-of-enchantment-legacy-fund-monies-go-to-nmda-natural-resource-programs.html)), and its principles include maximizing biodiversity and minimizing external inputs ([NMDA](https://nmdeptag.nmsu.edu/media/pdf/HSP-FAQ-for-INDIVIDUAL-APPLICANTS-FY26.pdf)). Every grant like that works better with someone on the ground who knows how to build soil biology. Each graduate of this workshop is one more of those people. We return to New Mexico in May 2027. <mark>[FIND: whether any graduates from this workshop work on Healthy Soil Program projects.]</mark>

Why it matters
Costa Rica is famous for bringing its forests back. Forest cover fell to 21 to 25 percent of the country in the 1980s and climbed back to 53 percent by 2016 ([Inter Press Service](https://www.ipsnews.net/?p=146239)). Its farmland is under heavy chemical pressure. FAO data put Costa Rica first in the world for pesticide use per hectare over 2000 to 2020, at about 23 kg per hectare ([Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/costa-rica-es-el-pais-que-utiliza-mas-plaguicidas-en-todo-el-mundo-segun-estadisticas-de-la-fao/)).
The cost falls on families, and on children first. Researchers at the National University in Heredia followed 451 pregnant women living near banana plantations in Matina and found levels of a fungicide breakdown product in their urine more than five times higher than in other populations studied ([van Wendel de Joode et al., 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4256696)). In Cartago, just east of San José, the fungicide chlorothalonil contaminated two rural water systems, and about 10,000 people have had drinking water delivered by tanker truck since October 2022 ([Delfino.cr](https://delfino.cr/2023/05/salud-aya-y-minae-recomiendan-prohibir-el-plaguicida-clorotalonil)). Soil biology offers farmers a way to keep yields up while relying less on chemicals: in a 2025 study of 56 farmer-managed fields, fields treated with compost had soil health scores 31 percent higher than reference fields, with no drop in wheat yields ([Edlinger et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/sae2.70041)). Schools are one place where that knowledge can spread. A 2022 survey by Costa Rica's Ministry of Public Education found garden projects in only 514 schools, 14.2 percent of those surveyed, while 2,404 schools said they would start one if they had support ([Ministerio de Educación Pública](https://mep.go.cr/sites/default/files/2024-07/ProgramaHuertasEstudiantiles.pdf)). Training consultants on a school campus with a working organic farm connects soil science directly to the next generation. <mark>[FIND: whether Armonía's school uses the compost or the farm in its classes, and whether any Costa Rican graduates now consult for local farms.]</mark>

Why Wild Ken Hill
Wild Ken Hill runs rewilding, regenerative farming and traditional conservation side by side. Its land stretches from the sea through coastal scrub, freshwater marshes, heathland and wood pasture to nature-rich farmland, and more than 2,500 species have been recorded there ([Workshop Handbook, June 2025](https://www.soilfoodweb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/WORKSHOP-HANDBOOK-JUNE-2025.pdf); [UK Accelerator Workshop page, FAQ](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)).
It is also a working example of what students came to learn. Farm manager Nick Padwick became the first British farmer to qualify as a Soil Food Web consultant, one of just 39 worldwide at the time ([Wild Ken Hill](https://wildkenhill.co.uk/bringing-the-soil-back-to-life/); [Wild Ken Hill](https://wildkenhill.co.uk/were-going-organic/)). He now makes about 750 tons of compost a year ([October 2025 newsletter](https://soilfoodweb.com/october-2025-newsletter/)), and compost has gone onto all of the estate's farmland ([Wild Ken Hill](https://wildkenhill.co.uk/compost-the-power-of/)). In one year his soil tests moved from a fungal-to-bacterial ratio of 0.01 to 0.6, heading toward the 1 to 1 balance he is aiming for ([Farmers Weekly](https://www.fwi.co.uk/arable/land-preparation/soils/how-composting-is-helping-organic-transition-on-a-norfolk-farm)). The farm has used no insecticide for over a decade and is converting its arable land to organic ([Wild Ken Hill](https://wildkenhill.co.uk/were-going-organic/)). In 2022 an independent assessment placed it in the top 5 percent of UK arable farms for carbon sequestration ([Wild Ken Hill](https://wildkenhill.co.uk/bringing-the-soil-back-to-life/)).
Students also had field instruction on Nick's farm and saw the same process working at farm scale ([October 2025 newsletter](https://soilfoodweb.com/october-2025-newsletter/)).
Why it matters
British farming relies heavily on bought-in nitrogen. The UK imports around 60 percent of the nitrogen fertilizer it uses ([AHDB](https://ahdb.org.uk/news/where-does-the-uk-import-fertiliser-from)), and the price of ammonium nitrate jumped from about £395 a tonne in 2021 to £650 to £850 in 2022 ([AHDB](https://ahdb.org.uk/news/fertiliser-prices-continue-to-climb); [AHDB](https://ahdb.org.uk/news/analyst-insight-industry-at-risk-as-cf-close-billingham)). Much of the excess washes into rivers: in the catchment of Norfolk's River Wensum, 40 percent of water bodies are at risk of exceeding the drinking water limit for nitrate ([University of East Anglia](https://www.uea.ac.uk/groups-and-centres/projects/wensum-alliance/the-wensum)). Meanwhile, arable soils in England and Wales have lost 40 to 60 percent of their organic carbon ([Environment Agency](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/state-of-the-environment/summary-state-of-the-environment-soil)). A farmer-led survey of over 1,300 hectares found that 42 percent of fields may be over-worked, judged by missing or rare earthworms ([Stroud, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0203909)).
Compost is one of the most reliable ways to rebuild that carbon: a global analysis of 1,972 comparisons found organic amendments raise cropland soil organic carbon by 26.9 percent on average ([Bai et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.catena.2023.107343)). Rebuilding the life in the soil answers several of these problems at once, and it spreads as fast as there are people who know how to do it. Every student who leaves an Accelerator Workshop has worked through all the learning outcomes for BioComplete™ Compost Production and Biological Liquid Amendments ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)), so they go home able to make biologically complete compost and liquid amendments, read what is happening under a microscope, and help the farms and gardens around them. Many groups keep supporting each other years after the workshop ends ([UK Accelerator Workshop page](https://soilfoodweb.com/jun2026-aw/)), which means the network of skilled practitioners keeps growing long after the ten days are over.

Why it matters
Costa Rican farms are some of the most input-intensive in the world. In 2022 the country used about 899 kg of fertilizer per hectare of arable land, close to seven times the world average of 135 kg ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/AG.CON.FERT.ZS?locations=CR)). Every bag is imported. When prices jumped 186 percent in 2022, the Minister of Agriculture said the country depends 100 percent on fertilizer imports, and farmers warned that for some crops fertilizer made up as much as 70 percent of production costs ([Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/mag-y-agricultores-advierten-el-pais-esta-a-las-puertas-de-una-caida-en-la-produccion-de-alimentos/)).
Costa Rican farmers already have a tradition of working with soil microbes. Many collect "microorganismos de montaña," beneficial microbes from the forest floor, and multiply them on the farm; the Ministry of Agriculture describes them as microorganisms that improve soil health and plant growth ([Ministerio de Agricultura y Ganadería](https://www.mag.go.cr/bibliotecavirtual/AV-0315.pdf)). Researchers at the University of Costa Rica have measured these preparations and found microbial counts of a million to a hundred million colony-forming units per milliliter ([Castro-Barquero & González-Acuña, 2021](https://www.revistas.ucr.ac.cr/index.php/agrocost/article/view/45703/45817)). The Accelerator Workshop builds on that local knowledge with the tools to see which organisms are present under a microscope, and to make compost that carries a full range of soil life. Every graduate can bring that skill to farms that are paying more and more for imported fertilizer. <mark>[FIND: what Armonía grows, and whether its farm has measured changes in its soil.]</mark>

Why it matters
Cyprus is running out of water. In early 2025, around the time of the workshop, the island's reservoirs stood at 26 percent of capacity, and by February 2026 they had fallen to 13.8 percent ([Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/02/11/reservoirs-remain-critical-at-13-8-per-cent-full)). Desalination now covers about 70 percent of urban drinking water needs ([FastForward](https://fastforward.com.cy/tech/water-scarcity-cyprus-desalination-enough)). The wider region is warming 20 percent faster than the rest of the world ([Union for the Mediterranean](https://ufmsecretariat.org/mediterraneanpavilion/first-scientific-report-on-climate-and-environmental-change-in-the-mediterranean/)).
The land is under the same pressure. According to an EU Joint Research Centre report, 45 percent of Cyprus's land meets the criteria for desertification, and 61 percent of Cypriots live in affected areas, the highest share in the EU ([Politis](https://en.politis.com.cy/news/environment/1023761/cyprus-land-desertification-ranks-worst-in-eu)). Cypriot soils average only 1.5 percent organic carbon, and a study in the Troodos mountains found just 1.0 percent on productive farmland ([Djuma et al., 2020](https://repository.cyi.ac.cy/bitstream/CyI/1000/1/Djuma_et_al_2020_The%20effect%20of%20agricultural%20abandonment%20and%20mountain%20terrace%20degradation.pdf)). Each 1 percent increase in soil organic matter lets the top foot of soil hold about 3,400 more gallons of plant-available water per acre ([University of Nebraska-Lincoln Water](https://water.unl.edu/article/animal-manure-management/connection-between-soil-organic-matter-and-soil-water/)). On an island this dry, building soil biology is also a way of storing water.
The Cypriot government is moving in the same direction. Its Ministry of Agriculture coordinates LIFE AgrOassis, an EU-funded project that aims to raise soil organic matter by 30 percent and build two compost facilities on the island ([European Commission](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/life/publicWebsite/project/LIFE21-CCA-CY-LIFE-AgrOassis-101074744/regenerative-approaches-for-building-climate-change-resilience-into-eu-agricultural-regions-prone-to-desertification)). Training people who know how to make biologically complete compost gives projects like this the skills they need. With students from 19 countries, the workshop also sent those skills to dry and degraded land around the world. <mark>[FIND: whether Riverland or Cypriot graduates have measured changes in their soil since.]</mark>

From cattle ranch back to forest
In February 2024 the Soil Food Web School ran a six-day, in-person Ecosystem Restoration Workshop in Mexico with Ecosystem Restoration Communities ([Beneath the Surface newsletter](https://soilfoodweb.com/beneath-the-surface-a-soil-food-web-school-newsletter/); [Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)). The training was first planned by the Ecosystem Restoration Foundation, and the two organizations agreed that the school would host it instead ([Enrollment Agreement, Mexico 2024](https://soilfoodweb.com/ecosystem-restoration-fundamentals-workshop-mexico-2024-v2/)).
It took place at the [Earth Connection Center](https://www.earthconnection.center/) Biological Reserve, 39 hectares of dry tropical forest on the Yucatán Peninsula, with a cenote and Mayan ruins on the land ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)). For many years the forest had been clear cut and used as a cattle ranch, which left the land exposed and heavily degraded ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)). Canadian ecologist Paul Morris and Mexican agronomist Sophia Ortiz founded the reserve in 2015 to restore it ([Climate Cleanup](https://v5.climatecleanup.org/en/news/planet-healers-at-the-resilient-ecosystem-institute)).
The land has answered. Since restoration began in 2016, the soil has turned brown, an organic layer has developed, and the tree canopy has started to close, so the soil holds more moisture ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)). Wildlife has returned in greater diversity and numbers, and the reserve's growing species list is one of its proudest achievements ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)). The team plants native species, is building a food forest and is testing other regenerative ways to grow food ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)). Their goal is to share what works with local Mayan communities and help restore thousands of hectares ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)).
What the workshop was for
The workshop was designed for students of the Introduction to Ecosystem Restoration course ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)), which the school created together with Ecosystem Restoration Communities ([Explorer.land](https://explorer.land/x/project/eccbr/about)). It gave them the chance to put the online course into practice with their own hands and to write a restoration plan for a small plot of land on site ([Enrollment Agreement, Mexico 2024](https://soilfoodweb.com/ecosystem-restoration-fundamentals-workshop-mexico-2024-v2/)). The aim was to give students the confidence, connections and know-how to take what they learned out into the world ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)).
What students did
Paul and Sophia took students through the steps of running a restoration project that can grow ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)). Over six days, participants:

* Assessed a healthy reference ecosystem ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500))
* Collected soil samples and other data to assess the health of a site ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500))
* Practised identifying species ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500))
* Learned tree and plant nursery skills ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500))
* Built habitat structures to increase biodiversity ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500))
* Wrote a restoration plan for a real site ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500))

They also discussed how to remove the pressures that degrade an ecosystem, how to raise money for restoration work and how to involve the local community ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)).
The team
Paul Morris has more than 25 years of experience in ecology, botany, ecosystem restoration and regenerative farming, and is a Certified Ecological Restoration Practitioner with the Society for Ecological Restoration ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)). Sophia Ortiz is an agronomy engineer who works with students on soil regeneration, local plant propagation and regenerative agriculture, and is developing a botanical garden of local species ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)). Why it matters
The forests of the Yucatán are disappearing fast. The peninsula lost 285,580 hectares of forest between 2019 and 2023, about 196 hectares a day, driven by agribusiness, cattle ranching, industrial pig and poultry farms, tourism and the Tren Maya railway ([Excélsior](https://www.excelsior.com.mx/nacional/peninsula-de-yucatan-perdio-cobertura-forestal-equivalente-a-dos-veces-la-cdmx/1650072)). Once the forest is gone, the soil goes with it. Most of Yucatán state has shallow, stony soils, and forming new soil takes hundreds to thousands of years ([CICY](https://www.cicy.mx/documentos/CICY/sitios/Biodiversidad/pdfs/Cap1/05%20Suelo.pdf)).
What happens to the soil also reaches the water. The people of the Yucatán drink from an aquifer reached through cenotes, and a 2025 study of ten cenotes found PFAS chemicals in eight, elevated E. coli in five and high nitrate levels in two ([Kopczynski et al., 2025](https://pfascentral.org/science/investigation-of-anthropogenic-and-emerging-contaminants-in-sinkholes-cenotes-of-the-great-mayan-aquifer-yucatan-peninsula)). Restoration that rebuilds living soil helps keep water and nutrients in the ground: soils treated with compost hold more nutrients and support more active microbial life ([Edlinger et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/sae2.70041)).
The region also holds one of the world's oldest models of farming with the forest. The Maya milpa has been managed in rotation for 3,500 years, with long fallows that let the forest grow back between plantings ([UNDP](https://stories.climateandforests-undp.org/heritage-of-the-milpa)), and in 2023 FAO recognized it as a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System ([El Financiero](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/opinion/alberto-munoz/2023/06/21/fao-reconocio-la-milpa-maya-peninsular-como-un-sistema-del-patrimonio-agricola-mundial/)). This workshop connected that heritage to a global movement. Ecosystem Restoration Communities is an official partner of the UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)). At the time of the workshop its network had more than 60 restoration initiatives ([Yucatán workshop page](https://promo.soilfoodweb.com/?p=106500)), and it now counts 86 initiatives in 39 countries, caring for more than 103,000 hectares of recovering land ([Resilience.org](https://www.resilience.org/stories/2026-02-13/healing-the-earth-one-community-at-a-time-highlighting-ercs-global-impact/)).

Where the Costa Rica workshops began
In August 2023, the Soil Food Web School brought its ten-day Accelerator Workshop to the metro area around San José ([workshop page](https://new.soilfoodweb.com/workshop/accelerator-workshop-august-2023/); [SFW workshops page](https://new.soilfoodweb.com/workshops/)). It is the earliest Costa Rica workshop on the school's list ([SFW workshops page](https://new.soilfoodweb.com/workshops/)), and the first of five Costa Rica workshops held or scheduled between 2023 and 2027 ([SFW workshops page](https://new.soilfoodweb.com/workshops/)). <mark>[CONFIRM: venue, and whether it was Armonía Agricultura Orgánica.]</mark>
San José sits in the Central Valley, home to about seven in ten Costa Ricans ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Valle-Central)). The valley's soils formed from the volcanic material and lava of four nearby volcanoes and are naturally rich ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Valle-Central)). Coffee farms replaced the valley's forests around 1850, and coffee growing since then has leached the soils and reduced their fertility ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Valle-Central)). It is a place where the soil once had everything, and where bringing its life back makes a real difference.
What the workshop was for
The workshop trained students from the school's consultant and grower training programs to make biological amendments by hand, working alongside the Soil Food Web team for ten days ([workshop page](https://new.soilfoodweb.com/workshop/accelerator-workshop-august-2023/)). Biological amendments such as compost return organic matter and active microbial life to soil, and the evidence for what that does is strong. In a 2025 study of 56 farmer-managed fields, fields treated with compost scored 31 percent higher on soil health than reference fields ([Edlinger et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/sae2.70041)). Their soils showed 45 percent more microbial respiration, a measure of how active soil life is, and 42 percent more capacity to hold on to nutrients, while wheat yields held steady ([Edlinger et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/sae2.70041)). <mark>[CONFIRM: number of graduates and their home countries; the graduation photo shows about 20.]</mark> <mark>[CONFIRM: what else students practised, such as microscopy and compost extracts.]</mark>
Why it matters
Costa Rica's food system leans heavily on imports. In 2021 the country imported about 99 percent of the maize it ate, 92 percent of its beans and 69 percent of its rice, which places it among the most import-dependent countries in the world for basic grains ([Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/costa-rica-esta-entre-los-paises-mas-dependientes-de-la-importacion-de-granos-basicos/)). Farmers also depend on imported fertilizer. When Russia's invasion of Ukraine sent fertilizer prices sharply higher in 2022, farmers across Central America saw their production costs spike, wrote former Costa Rican president Carlos Alvarado Quesada ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/27/agricultural-subsidies-fertilizer-food-security-just-rural-transition/)).
The soils that feed the country are under strain. The slopes of the Irazú volcano in Cartago, just east of San José, grow about 80 percent of Costa Rica's vegetables ([Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/preocupa-erosion-de-los-suelos-volcanicos-donde-se-cultiva-80-de-las-hortalizas-del-pais/)). Researchers measured erosion there at an average of 50 tonnes of soil per hectare per year, and up to 90 tonnes in years of extreme rain ([Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/preocupa-erosion-de-los-suelos-volcanicos-donde-se-cultiva-80-de-las-hortalizas-del-pais/)). University of Costa Rica geographer Víctor Cortés says farmers in the area rely more and more on machinery and fertilizer as the land's productivity falls ([Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/preocupa-erosion-de-los-suelos-volcanicos-donde-se-cultiva-80-de-las-hortalizas-del-pais/)).
Farmers in Costa Rica are already looking for another way. About 5,500 farmers have learned to make their own biological inputs from farm waste and forest soil microbes, through training run by the Ministry of Agriculture, the national training institute INA and the state distance university UNED ([AmeliaRueda.com](https://ameliarueda.com/nota/agricultores-bioinsumos-agroquimicos-nacionales-costa-rica)). Some cut their agrochemical costs by more than 40 percent ([AmeliaRueda.com](https://ameliarueda.com/nota/agricultores-bioinsumos-agroquimicos-nacionales-costa-rica)). In September 2026 the Ministry of Agriculture launched a national bioinputs strategy for 2026 to 2035, with training for producers and extension workers as one of its four main lines of work ([La Región](https://laregion.cr/costa-rica-lanza-estrategia-nacional-de-bioinsumos-para-transformar-la-agricultura/)). At the same time, certified organic farmland fell 8.6 percent between 2024 and 2025, according to state plant health data cited by the ecological federation Fecon ([Delfino.cr](https://delfino.cr/2026/02/federacion-ecologista-se-han-perdido-1800-hectareas-de-produccion-organica-durante-la-actual-administracion)). Programs like these need skilled local people who can teach soil biology.
