Our story

It began with one scientist looking closer.

Dr. Elaine Ingham pioneered research in soil biology across forty-five years and made her science radically accessible, so that anyone anywhere could learn to restore the soil beneath their feet.

The Foundation was created in 2025 to carry that teaching forward. She passed in February 2026, and the work she started continues through the Foundation, the school inside it, and practitioners in more than 132 countries who bring the same close attention to their own soil.

She taught at Oregon State from 1986 to 2002, served as the first President of the Soil Ecology Society, and co-authored the USDA's Soil Biology Primer.

Why this matters for climatePlants pull carbon from the air and invest much of it in the soil, where it is built into microbial bodies, aggregates and long-lived fungal networks. Biological management also avoids emissions, because less fertilizer has to be made and moved, and the field needs fewer passes.

Practice

Growers are already doing this, at every scale.

The biology is universal; the practice is local. Every result we publish names the grower who got it and the year they did.

Nick Padwick farms four thousand acres in Norfolk, England. Roberto Silva works a hundred and forty in Piura, Peru. Jenn Sirp grows on a quarter acre in California. They film the work themselves and publish what changed, so nobody has to take our word for it.

The scale runs from that quarter acre to ten thousand acres of corn and soy in Illinois, and a public park on Governors Island in New York. The steps are the same at every size: look at the biology under a microscope, add what is missing, and measure again.

We partner with growers to trial the approach where they farm, and to document what happens when they do.
