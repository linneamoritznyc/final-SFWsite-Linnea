Accelerator Workshop October 2025

Área Metropolitana de San José, Costa Rica · 13–24 October 2025

This workshop has ended.

A third visit in fifteen months

The October 2025 Accelerator Workshop brought the Soil Food Web School back to Armonía Agricultura Orgánica in Heredia, in the San José metro area of Costa Rica, for the third time in 15 months, after July 2024 and March 2025 (SFW workshops page; October 2025 newsletter). It was the school's fourth in-person workshop of 2025, after Cyprus in January, Costa Rica in March and Norfolk in June (October 2025 newsletter). Coming back to the same host again and again shows a partnership that works for both sides.

Armonía is a campus built around health and learning. It includes primary and secondary schools, and its organic farm serves as an educational centerpiece and feeds visitors at an on-site restaurant (October 2025 newsletter). Armonía's stated mission is to become an example project of organic product management, showing ecological responsibility and supplying locally grown food (Workshop Handbook, March 2025). Students learned to make biological soil amendments on a farm where children grow up learning where food comes from.

What students did

The Accelerator Workshop is a ten-day, in-person learning pathway through the hands-on stages of our advanced training, equivalent to the regular pathway (Enrollment Agreement, June 2025). In the standard format, the group builds several compost piles at the same time, some designed to succeed and some designed to fail, so students learn the principles at play and how to rescue a failing pile (Workshop Handbook, June 2025). Students also run nematode extractions and protozoan infusions, make compost extracts and teas, and use applied microscopy to assess their compost and liquid amendments at key stages (Workshop Handbook, June 2025). Together these cover the learning outcomes for what are now the BioComplete™ Compost Production and Biological Liquid Amendments courses (UK Accelerator Workshop page, FAQ).

Why it matters

Costa Rica is famous for bringing its forests back. Forest cover fell to 21 to 25 percent of the country in the 1980s and climbed back to 53 percent by 2016 (Inter Press Service). Its farmland is under heavy chemical pressure. FAO data put Costa Rica first in the world for pesticide use per hectare over 2000 to 2020, at about 23 kg per hectare (Semanario Universidad).

The cost falls on families, and on children first. Researchers at the National University in Heredia followed 451 pregnant women living near banana plantations in Matina and found levels of a fungicide breakdown product in their urine more than five times higher than in other populations studied (van Wendel de Joode et al., 2014). In Cartago, just east of San José, the fungicide chlorothalonil contaminated two rural water systems, and about 10,000 people have had drinking water delivered by tanker truck since October 2022 (Delfino.cr). Costa Rica banned chlorothalonil in November 2023 (Delfino.cr).

Soil biology offers farmers a way to keep yields up while relying less on chemicals: in a 2025 study of 56 farmer-managed fields, fields treated with compost had soil health scores 31 percent higher than reference fields, with no drop in wheat yields (Edlinger et al., 2025). Schools are one place where that knowledge can spread. A 2022 survey by Costa Rica's Ministry of Public Education found garden projects in only 514 schools, 14.2 percent of those surveyed, while 2,404 schools said they would start one if they had support (Ministerio de Educación Pública). Training consultants on a school campus with a working organic farm connects soil science directly to the next generation.
