Accelerator Workshop March 2025

Área Metropolitana de San José, Costa Rica · 3–14 March 2025

This workshop has ended.

The whole group with the compost pile they built together

Overview

The March 2025 Accelerator Workshop took place at Armonía Agricultura Orgánica in La Asunción de Belén, Heredia, about 11 km north of San José, Costa Rica (Workshop Handbook March 2025; RegeneraVida). The farm's stated mission is to "become an example project of organic product management" (Workshop Handbook March 2025). It is warm all year, and March falls in the dry season (Workshop Handbook March 2025). Students worked in covered and open outdoor workspaces and used indoor classrooms for microscopy, and lunch was served on site from Armonía's organic cafe (Workshop Handbook March 2025). The site has a large carved "Hacienda" sign (photo), a classroom with orange walls (photo) and a long tiled veranda.

The ten days followed a clear arc (Workshop Handbook March 2025). On day one, students learned the feedstock categories, wrote their compost recipes and hydrated their materials (Workshop Handbook March 2025). Photos from that day show them kneeling on the floor to sort green leaves by hand and digging into big sacks of feedstock (photo). On day two they built their first pile and took soil samples (Workshop Handbook March 2025). Microscopy of soil and compost started on day three, extracts and brewers on day four, and the first pile turn on day five (Workshop Handbook March 2025). The second week moved to compost teas, nematode extraction, protozoan infusions and humic acid extraction, and closed with a certificate ceremony (Workshop Handbook March 2025). Mentor Gerald Ramirez taught biological liquid amendments (2025 in Review; photo), and students and mentors spent long sessions together at the microscope (2025 in Review; photo). The students and mentors also "came up with some great techniques for mixing compost materials" (October 2025 newsletter).

The staff and mentors posed for their own group photo in the orange-walled classroom (photo).

Why it matters

The San José area lies in the Costa Rican seasonal moist forests ecoregion, a band of forest between wet and dry zones (One Earth). San José sits in the middle of it (Wikipedia), and most of the country's people live there (One Earth). Less than 11 percent of its original forest remains (One Earth). Over the past century, lowlands were cleared for cattle and mountain slopes for beans, corn, coffee and dairy (One Earth). Soil health here affects more people than anywhere else in the country. Students spent ten days in the middle of it, learning to make biological amendments that can replace part of the chemical inputs Costa Rican farms rely on (Workshop Handbook March 2025; Semanario Universidad).
