Accelerator Workshop October 2026

Tamil Nadu, India · 19–30 October 2026

Overview

The October 2026 Accelerator Workshop takes place in Tamil Nadu, in southern India (India workshop page). Students stay at the Isha Yoga Center, at the foot of the Velliangiri Mountains, about 30 km west of Coimbatore (India workshop page). A daily shuttle takes them about 3 km to Sadhguru's Save Soil Regenerative Revolution Farm, where the hands-on work happens (India workshop page). The workshop is run together with Isha Outreach, part of the Isha Foundation, and the Conscious Planet Save Soil movement (India workshop page).

Over ten days, students work in small groups, each with an assigned instructor (India workshop page). In the first week they build thermophilic BioComplete™ compost piles from scratch: choosing feedstocks, balancing carbon and nitrogen, writing their own recipes, then monitoring, turning and troubleshooting each pile (India workshop page). The weekend is free, with optional compost monitoring (India workshop page). In the second week they make compost extracts and teas at small and large scale, protozoan infusions and nematode extractions, take soil samples, assess fields, design experiments and apply what they made in the field (India workshop page). Microscopy on compost, soil and liquids runs through both weeks (India workshop page).

The instructors are Loida Vasquez, Director of Advanced Education; Gerald Ramirez, mentor and agronomist; Evan Buckman, Executive Director of the Soil Food Web Foundation; and Kavi Reddy, who leads growth, partnerships and permaculture (India workshop page). Students arrive having completed the four Foundation Courses or the two-day Life in the Soils seminar (India workshop page).

Why it matters

This is the school's first Accelerator Workshop in India (SFW workshops page). It feels special. So much of the heart of this organization relates to India, because our mission is so closely linked to food security (Soil Food Web Foundation), and India is where that link is sharpest. India has more undernourished people than any other country: more than 172 million in 2024, about 12 percent of the population (Down To Earth). 18.7 percent of Indian children under five suffer from wasting, the highest rate in the world (Down To Earth). At the same time, 97.85 million hectares of India, 29.7 percent of the country's land, were degraded in 2018 to 2019, up from 28.76 percent in 2003 to 2005 (Down To Earth).

India is also where farming that works with soil biology has grown fastest. In Andhra Pradesh, the state's Community Managed Natural Farming programme (APCNF) (Instagram @apzbnf) has brought natural farming to 1.8 million farmers through 340,000 women's self-help groups in more than 8,000 villages (Food Planet Prize). In 2026 it won the Food Planet Prize, which describes itself as the world's largest environmental award (Food Planet Prize). The UN Environment Programme reports at least a 30 percent increase in productivity and income for farmers who adopted it (UNEP). A 2026 analysis of the programme's soil science builds on Dr. Elaine Ingham's soil food web research (Biodiversity for a Livable Climate), and Didi Pershouse, who teaches the school's Soil Sponge Regeneration Workshop, has worked as a trainer and curriculum developer for APCNF (Soil Sponge Regeneration Workshop page).

Our host partner, the Conscious Planet Save Soil movement, was launched by Sadhguru to turn soil loss around through national soil policy (Conscious Planet). In 2022 he rode a motorcycle alone for 100 days and 30,000 km, from London's Trafalgar Square to India, through 27 nations (Conscious Planet), and the campaign reached over 3.9 billion people, with 74 nations offering support (Sadhguru blog). The movement asks governments to require a minimum of 3 to 6 percent organic content in agricultural soils, and Gujarat became the first Indian state to sign a Save Soil agreement (Free Press Journal). Its case rests on a United Nations warning that over 90 percent of the earth's soil could become degraded by 2050 (Conscious Planet). The Accelerator Workshop follows the school's two-day Life in the Soils seminar, held at the same Save Soil Farm on 16 and 17 October with the Isha Foundation and Conscious Planet (Life in the Soils seminar page). Together they give farmers and consultants in India the hands-on skills to build soil organic content back.
