Accelerator Workshop July 2024

Área Metropolitana de San José, Costa Rica · 15–26 July 2024

This workshop has ended.

The start of a partnership

The July 2024 Accelerator Workshop took place at Armonía Agricultura Orgánica, an organic farm and school campus in La Asunción de Belén, Heredia (Workshop Handbook, March 2025; October 2025 newsletter). Armonía has outdoor workspaces, both covered and open, and indoor classrooms for microscopy (Workshop Handbook, March 2025). Its mission is to become an example project of organic product management, showing ecological responsibility and supplying locally grown food (Workshop Handbook, March 2025).

The partnership worked: Armonía hosted the school twice more in 2025 (October 2025 newsletter).

What the workshop was for

The school invited students from its consultant and grower training programs to "participate in a hands-on composting workshop to advance their skills in creating biologically complete compost" (Beneath the Surface newsletter). It brought together the people who will advise farms and the people who run them.

The workshop ran in July, during Heredia's rainy season from May to October (Workshop Handbook, March 2025), so students learned to manage piles in wet conditions. That is a skill many of them will need at home.

What students said

The best sign of what this workshop did comes from the students. One wrote: "Excellent all round. Great exchange of experiences and ideas. Learned lots. Am now ready to take consultancy course" (July 2024 student, quoted on the UK Accelerator Workshop page). Another said: "Greatly enjoyed it and glad I took the training at the start of the CTP" (July 2024 student, quoted on the UK Accelerator Workshop page). For a funder, that first line is the outcome that matters: a student who left ready to go on and advise farms.

Why it matters

Armonía is a working farm in a place where farmland is disappearing. La Asunción belongs to the northern industrial zone of the canton of Belén, with about 2,210 people per square kilometer (IGES). Across the Central Valley, coffee land shrank from 18,241 hectares in 2001 to 13,699 hectares in 2014, and real estate pressure is one of the main causes, with Belén and Heredia among the places where coffee is disappearing (La Nación).

What happens on the land around Heredia also reaches people's taps. The Barva aquifer helps supply drinking water to San José, Heredia and Alajuela. Forest cover above it fell from 30 percent in 1979 to 13 percent in 2019, and nitrate levels at lower elevations have reached 68 mg per liter, well above the 25 mg alert level (Semanario Universidad). Living soil holds on to nutrients: in one study, fields treated with compost had 42 percent more capacity to hold nutrients than reference fields (Edlinger et al., 2025). Training consultants and growers on one of the last farms in an industrial canton keeps that knowledge in the community that needs it most.
