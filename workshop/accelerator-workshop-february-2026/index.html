Accelerator Workshop February 2026

Santa Fe, United States · 23 Feb – 6 Mar 2026

CONFIRM: names of the mentors in the purple shirts

The February 2026 Accelerator Workshop took place at Synergia Ranch, about 15 miles south of Santa Fe, New Mexico. (Wikipedia) The ranch was founded in 1969. (Wikipedia) Its 111 acres sit in high-altitude, semi-arid piñon-juniper savannah, with a 400-tree orchard planted in 1976 and no-dig vegetable gardens. (Synergia Ranch) Photos from the workshop show dry grassland with cholla cactus, adobe domes, and cottonwood trees still bare in late winter.

Much of the work happened outdoors. Students inspected feedstocks in open blue barrels, leaning in over the dry stems while a mentor talked the group through what each material would add to a pile. Some lessons were taught standing outside, with the whole group in the sun and the hills behind them. Indoors, students followed lectures in front of projected slides and worked at the microscope. Mentors in purple SFW shirts led the workshop. In the evenings the group gathered on benches around a fire pit under the bare trees.

The workshop ended with graduates in purple workshop shirts holding up their certificates around a long table.

CONFIRM: number of students and countries, and a student quote with permission.

Why it matters

This was the school's dryland workshop. The country south of Santa Fe lies where four ecoregions meet: the Southern Rocky Mountains, the Arizona/New Mexico Mountains, the Southwestern Tablelands and the Arizona/New Mexico Plateau. (Santa Fe Conservation Trust) The region has lived through repeated droughts, including 2000 to 2004 and 2021. (Conservation Biology Institute) Synergia Ranch left most of its land to recover from early overgrazing and uses drip irrigation to save water. (Synergia Ranch) In 2019 New Mexico passed its Healthy Soil Act, which funds farmers and ranchers to raise soil organic matter, microbial activity and water retention, and measures soil microbiology as part of its baseline. (SiX Agriculture) Students here built soil biology in the conditions the state is now investing in.
