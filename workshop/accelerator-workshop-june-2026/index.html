Accelerator Workshop June 2026

Norfolk, United Kingdom · 15–26 June 2026

This workshop has ended. Leave your details to hear about the next one.

Overview

Dates
15 to 26 June 2026
Status
Closed
Host
Wild Ken Hill · Instagram @wildkenhill_norfolk
Instructors
Nick Padwick (host) CONFIRM: the mentors who led the teams

The June 2026 Accelerator Workshop took place at Wild Ken Hill Estate, at Heacham Bottom Farm near Snettisham in Norfolk, England. (June 2026 workshop page; Workshop Handbook June 2025) The estate covers about 4,000 acres, running from the sea through coastal scrub, freshwater marshes, heathland and wood pasture to farmland, and combines rewilding, regenerative farming and traditional conservation. (Wild Ken Hill; Workshop Handbook June 2025) More than 2,500 species have been recorded there. (Wild Ken Hill) The farm is managed by SFW School mentor Nick Padwick (October 2025 newsletter), and this was the school's second workshop there, one year after June 2025. (Workshop Handbook June 2025)

All 25 places sold out. (June 2026 workshop page) Students worked in teams of four or five, each team with its own instructor. (June 2026 workshop page) Over ten days the teams built eight compost piles by hand in a barn on the estate. (June 2026 workshop page) (Linnea Moritz, attended the workshop) Every team measured its ingredients and wrote its own recipe, then monitored, turned and charted its pile for the rest of the workshop. (Linnea Moritz, attended the workshop) Because the eight piles stood side by side, students could compare their own pile with the piles of the other teams and see how each recipe choice played out. (Linnea Moritz, attended the workshop) Students also made compost extracts and teas, ran nematode extractions and protozoan infusions, checked their soil, compost and liquids under the microscope, and plotted a sampling grid for field work. (June 2026 workshop page)

The teams built their piles in tall wire mesh bins. One pile was built as a tribute to Dr. Elaine Ingham, who died in February 2026, and the finished pile was covered in flowers in her memory. (Linnea Moritz, attended the workshop)

CONFIRM: number of countries students came from, number of graduates, and a student quote with permission.

Why it matters

Wild Ken Hill is one of England's best-known places where rewilding and food production share the same land. (Rewilding Britain; Wild Ken Hill) Since 2019, about 1,000 acres of poor-quality farmland on the estate have been rewilded, grazed at low density by Red Poll cattle, Tamworth pigs and Exmoor ponies. (Rewilding Britain) Within three years, the average number of plant species in survey plots roughly doubled, from 16.8 to 33.2, and rare plants such as smooth cat's-ear spread. (BirdGuides) Two pairs of beavers released in 2020 produced Norfolk's first beaver kit in about 600 years in 2021. (Forth 1) Students built soil biology on a working farm surrounded by recovering wild land.
