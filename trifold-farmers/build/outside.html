Why biology

Feed the organisms that feed your plants.

Plants don't feed alone.

Bacteria and fungi produce enzymes that break down minerals and organic matter and free the nutrients they contain. When protozoa and nematodes consume those bacteria and fungi, the surplus is released right at the root, in plant-available form.

Bacteria produce glues that bind soil particles into microaggregates; fungal hyphae weave those into larger macroaggregates. The result behaves like a sponge, holding water, letting air flow in and resisting erosion.

These microbes are often lost in land that has been chemically managed or compacted. Biologically complete compost is designed to restore them.

Our storyDr. Elaine Ingham mapped the Soil Food Web and taught the world to restore it. The Soil Food Web Foundation, established in October 2025, carries her work forward with growers on working land.

The soil beneath our farms and forests is alive.

A web of bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes and microarthropods feeds plants, builds soil, stores carbon and cleans water when it is intact.

We train farmers, and the people who work with them, to transition from chemical inputs to biology. You'll learn to test your soil, produce your own BioComplete™ compost and liquid amendments, and rebalance the microbial content of your fields. Living soil is the solution, and it is beneath your feet.
